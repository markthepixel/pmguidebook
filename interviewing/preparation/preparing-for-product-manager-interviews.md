# Preparing for Product Manager Interviews

Preparing for a Product Manager interview starts with understanding the work the company needs someone to do. A job title alone may not tell you whether the role focuses on discovery, growth, a technical platform, or leading an established product. Review the role description and the company's product, then ask the recruiter or hiring contact about the interview stages and what each discussion will cover. Use this information to select truthful, relevant examples of your work and identify areas where you need more practice.

## Example

Imagine you're preparing for a PM role at a company that provides inventory software for retailers. The role description emphasizes helping new customers set up their inventory, but it also mentions responsibility for an integration with accounting software. You explore the available product information and ask the hiring contact how these responsibilities fit together. You learn that the first priority is understanding why customers abandon setup, and that a later discussion will cover working with engineers on the integration.

You choose an example from your own experience where you investigated a confusing customer process. You make notes about what you knew at the beginning, how you gathered information, which options you considered, and what you personally decided. You distinguish your contribution from the team's work and include what the result did and didn't establish. If you haven't owned an integration before, you prepare to explain a related technical collaboration and acknowledge the limits of that experience rather than imply that you've done the exact job.

You also confirm the practical details of the process. If there is a take-home exercise, you ask about the expected time commitment, deliverable, and rules for using AI or other tools before beginning. You prepare questions about the team's access to customers and how success in the role will be evaluated. These questions help you investigate the opportunity as well as prepare answers for the interview.

## Pain Points

Preparing only from a job title or an online list of interview questions can leave you practicing for a different role. It can also be tempting to make your experience sound closer to the requirements than it actually is. You may not be able to confirm every detail of the process, so separate what the company has told you from assumptions you still need to check. Don't share confidential information from a current or former employer to make an example more convincing.

## Practical Exercise

Choose a PM role description and one experience that relates to its responsibilities. Write a short outline of how you would explain your own decisions and what you learned. Where does your experience fit the role, and what would you need to clarify with the hiring team? If you haven't held a PM role, use relevant project or team experience.
